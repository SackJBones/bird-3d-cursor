# Sphere-derived range and responsive direction

Runtime follow-up: [Lab 08's optional sphere-vector filter](SPHERE-FILTER-LAB.md)
uses a common radial/angular gain and filters wrist turns rather than carrying
the latest palm rotation through unfiltered. It retains the **original** range
polynomial; the ninth-power curve below remains an illustrative study only.
The [reference audit](RANGE-REFERENCE-AUDIT.md) confirms that the original
polynomial already has the desired qualitative progression and is the baseline
for future tuning. Input normalization and limit blending warrant investigation
before replacing that curve.

Design study, 2026-09-27. No production filter, scene, device or default changed.
The latest physical feedback concerns **VRChat lab, FILTERED**, not standalone
Quest. Dana reports RAW is responsive but too noisy. Lab 05 remains the last
deployed diagnostic scene; Lab 07's optional adaptive filter is build-only.

## User requirements

Dana's correction is authoritative:

> please just use sphere size/the position of the sphere center as the measure of hand "openness" and don't introduce a new concept or math quantity

Use the existing sphere geometry. Do not introduce joint-curl percentages, a
hand-openness classifier, or another pose-derived reach signal. The percentages
below describe desired feel, not quantities to compute or threshold:

- The first roughly 65% of comfortable opening should remain within arm's reach
  or a few steps, supporting quiet, smooth, fine positioning among small items.
- Roughly 65-80% should accelerate through architectural scales: moving a sofa
  onto a balcony, then manipulating a house.
- Roughly 90-100% should cover landscape scales and effectively infinite reach;
  sustaining such a wide hand is strenuous and should be needed infrequently.
- Repointing with fingers or wrist should realign quickly at every distance.
  RAW already achieves this responsiveness; FILTERED currently lags badly far
  away. Preserve useful noise rejection without range-dependent angular delay.
- Prefer a principled continuous design over a collection of pose bands or
  local patches. VRChat delivery remains the priority; bound this research.

Existing singular-fit/fist safety handling is a separate concern. Its current
implementation is not permission to repurpose its bend/flatness weights as a
new reach control. The study consumes the existing sphere-derived `rangeInput`;
private replay includes that vector's previously applied geometric limit law.
It neither refits recorded joints nor validates those existing limit choices.

## Candidate architecture

The existing center-minus-hand vector already has a length and direction.
Filter those separately **before** expanding length into Bird reach. Use the
current hand-derived palm frame to carry history with rigid wrist motion. No
head/torso reference is involved. Geometry still specifies one point; cursor
artwork, UI contact and scene snapping remain downstream policies.

Angular smoothing should depend on angular motion, rather than metres travelled
by a distant cursor. Range smoothing operates on the existing sphere-center
distance. Both adaptive responses use incoming sample motion, not the delayed
displayed Bird. The independently written study uses the speed-adaptive
low-pass principle described on the authors' [1 Euro filter page](https://gery.casiez.net/1euro/):
stronger smoothing at low speed, less lag during deliberate fast motion.
This is a useful basis, not evidence that these settings suit VRChat tracking.

Averaging the unexpanded Cartesian vector alone is insufficient: a turn makes
the averaged vector shorter, and the nonlinear range law magnifies that
shortening. Great-circle direction interpolation with a separately filtered
length preserves distance during a pure turn. The plot generated by the study
isolates this effect with matched constant filter gains.

Current-palm transport also carries palm-pose noise. It is not a complete
denoising solution. A production candidate must account for that noise, preserve
ordinary nearby precision and handle degeneracy without a direction jump.

## One illustrative range curve

Let `s` be the existing sphere-center distance in metres. The legacy polynomial
is `s + s^2/0.02 + 0.02*(s/0.03)^6`. The study retains its two low-order terms
and varies a single global power-law tail:

`F(s) = s + s^2/0.02 + 99.16*(s/0.12)^9`.

The illustrative constraints are at most 3 m reach at 8 cm sphere-center
distance, and 100 m at 12 cm. The ninth power is the smallest odd integer power
satisfying those constraints. These distances have **not** been calibrated to
Dana's subjective opening percentages and are **not** proposed new defaults.
There are no pose bands, joins or finite reach caps in this curve. It is
strictly increasing, reaches zero at the fist endpoint, and has unit gain at
the origin. Its radial 3D map retains the legacy quadratic term's C1 behavior
at zero; it is not claimed to be an everywhere analytic/conformal map.

| Sphere-center distance | Legacy reach | Study reach |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 2 cm | 0.04176 m | 0.04001 m |
| 4 cm | 0.2324 m | 0.1250 m |
| 7 cm | 3.543 m | 1.091 m |
| 8 cm | 7.592 m | 2.979 m |
| 12 cm | 82.76 m | 100 m |

The derivative is no greater than legacy throughout 0-8 cm, so the curve does
not increase local radial noise amplification there. Beyond that, it becomes
steeper. Unbounded reach necessarily has sensitivity costs; filtering and
intentional object snapping still matter. At the existing extreme 2 m input it
would produce about 9.84e12 m, demanding float/finite-math and downstream audits
before any runtime use. Effective infinity does not require blindly storing
arbitrarily large float coordinates.

## Evidence and limits

Run [the numerical study](../../tests/study_sphere_space_control.py) with Python
and NumPy; Matplotlib is optional for its plot:

```text
python tests/study_sphere_space_control.py --out <local-results> --plot
```

Synthetic results are retained in
[sphere-space-study.json](measurements/sphere-space-study.json) and
[sphere-range-law.csv](measurements/sphere-range-law.csv). Checks passed for
monotonicity, anchors, zero endpoint, rigid/mirrored transport and 30/72/120 Hz
direction steps across six sphere distances. At 72 Hz:

- A 90 or 170 degree direction step reaches 90% of its target in one sample
  (13.9 ms), independent of range; a 1 degree adjustment takes four (55.6 ms).
- Exact 180 degree reversal takes five samples (69.4 ms). Its shortest-path axis
  is nonunique; a deterministic test convention is not a finished interaction
  design. All tested pure turns preserve range to numerical precision.
- With synthetic independent 0.2 mm sphere-distance noise, radial RMS at 7 cm
  falls from 22.1 mm to 7.1 mm. At 12 cm it falls from 1.53 m to 0.485 m.
  Synthetic 0.05 degree angular noise is reduced to about 0.025 degrees.
  These are fixture inputs, not measured headset tracking noise or feel ratings.

Optional private replay accepts `--joint-trace <local-jsonl>`. The existing
OpenXR recording produced finite outputs for 1,440 right-hand and two left-hand
tracked samples; untracked data and long gaps reset history. It provides no VRChat ground
truth, useful left-hand coverage, static-noise benchmark or opening calibration.
Raw recordings and replay results remain private under ignored Validation.

This is double-precision numerical evidence only. There is no new Unity/Udon
implementation, compilation, headset performance measurement or physical test.
In particular, separate angular/radial gains are anisotropic: unit gain in the
range curve alone does not establish locally Euclidean smoothing. Near-origin
behavior, current-frame noise, tracking gaps/source changes, antipodes, float
precision and hand-size scaling remain production design checks.

## Bounded next step

Prototype the sphere-based filter as a separate optional policy in the VRChat
lab, preserving RAW and original FILTERED comparisons. First retain the existing
range law to isolate the filter's contribution; expose a separate explicit
range-curve comparison only after core checks. Assess near-hand trajectories,
raw-intent closing/opening, rigid wrist turns, fingertip-driven turns, mirroring,
loss/recovery and saved-scene compiled Udon behavior. Prevent radial contraction
during a pure turn, avoid angular latency growing with range, and keep full-fist
return exact. Preserve ordinary nearby behavior rather than introducing an
arbitrary hand-opening threshold to hide a coordinate singularity.

Do not promote a default based on these synthetic results. Seek a useful
physical comparison once a bounded implementation passes; if unresolved edge
cases require prolonged research, retain the notes and continue the agreed
VRChat work instead of getting marooned here.
